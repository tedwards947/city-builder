Toolbar concepts

Three ways to replace the flat row of text buttons with an icon bar under the stats / RCI panel. All three are driven by the same tool list, so a new building is one new entry. The mocks work: click around. Dashed, faded slots are made-up future tools, there only to show where growth goes.

Icon style applies to all three:

A · Palette with fly-outs (closest to SC2000)

Two columns of icon tiles. A tile with a corner tick is a family: click it and its members fly out to the left. The tile then shows the member you last picked, so a second click re-arms it without opening anything.

  • Smallest footprint; the map stays clear
  • A new tool joins a fly-out; a new family is one more tile
  • Most like the game you named
  • Family tiles change picture, which can confuse at first
  • Icons alone must carry meaning until you hover

B · Category rail with a drawer

One column of labelled categories. Clicking one opens a drawer of cards, each with a picture, name and price. The drawer stays open while you build and scrolls when a category gets long.

  • Friendliest to new players: names and prices are always visible
  • Room for clay thumbnails at a readable size
  • Grows furthest: long categories scroll, cards can show locks or stats
  • The open drawer covers more of the map
  • Always two clicks to switch family

C · Open shelf

Every tool is always visible, four across, under small group headings. One click for anything. Headings fold to save room.

  • Fastest: nothing is hidden, no fly-outs
  • You see the whole game at a glance
  • Tallest; with many more buildings it has to scroll or fold
  • Less SC2000, more "tool shed"

Common to all three

New / Save / Load move into a menu tile. Overlays (traffic, crime, fire risk, vibe) become one "layers" family, since only one shows at a time. Budget, undo and redo are single tiles. Hovering any tile shows its name, price and upkeep in the strip at the bottom left (in the game this would be the existing tooltip). Zone icons keep the R / C / I colours from the demand chart.
